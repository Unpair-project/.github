# UNPAIR - Understanding Parasite-Host Networks

## About the Unpair project

The UNPAIR project is coordinated at the Oswaldo Cruz Foundation (Fiocruz), Brazil, and is funded by the Brazilian National Council for Scientific and Technological Development (CNPq). It brings together an interdisciplinary research team comprising researchers from Fiocruz and international collaborators from Universidad Carlos III de Madrid and Universidad Complutense de Madrid, Spain.

**UNPAIR** is part of a broader network of research projects under the umbrella of [SESAM – Socioecological Systems in the Amazon](https://github.com/sesam-sinbiose), a scientific knowledge synthesis initiative coordinated by Fiocruz (Brazil) and integrated into the SinBiose-CNPq centre. SESAM promotes interdisciplinary and transdisciplinary research to understand how environmental changes and territorial dynamics influence biodiversity, ecosystem services and zoonotic disease risks in the Amazon.

**UNPAIR** aims to address knowledge gaps in parasite-host interactions by constructing local networks and regional meta-networks across Brazilian biomes and ecoregions. The project investigates how environmental, climatic, land-use and socioeconomic factors shape the structure and dynamics of these interactions.

By integrating ecological network analysis with information on host immunological characteristics and parasite virulence factors, UNPAIR seeks to advance our understanding of the mechanisms underlying parasite sharing and the emergence of zoonotic interactions.

Using empirical data and advanced statistical approaches, the project aims to characterise patterns of network structure and dynamics, identify areas of high beta-diversity, and predict how these patterns may change under future climate change and anthropogenic pressure scenarios.

Through this integrated approach, UNPAIR aims to contribute to biodiversity conservation, environmental management and public health within a One Health framework.

## Main research topics

- Parasite-host interaction networks
- Ecological networks and meta-networks
- Beta-diversity of species and interactions
- Host immunological characteristics
- Parasite virulence factors
- Environmental and climatic drivers
- Land-use and socioeconomic change
- Predictive modelling
- Biodiversity conservation and One Health
